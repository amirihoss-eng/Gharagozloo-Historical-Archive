## Short biography

Haji Abdollah Khan Gharagozloo, known at different stages as **Sa'ed al-Saltaneh**, **Sardar Akram**, and **Amir Nezam**, was a senior Qajar military commander, provincial administrator, landholder, and author in the Amir Nezam branch of the Hajilou Gharagozloo family. He was the son of Mostafa Qoli Khan E'temad al-Saltaneh and father of Hossein Qoli Khan, Mohtaj Ali Khan, and Mansur Ali Khan.

The archive now documents him not only through genealogical and office references, but through his own surviving writings. His reports reveal a military-administrative figure who paid close attention to frontier defense, troop organization, roads, water systems, agriculture, settlement, trade, and local political authority.

## Life and career — currently checkpointed

### Sarakhs frontier mission — 1294–1296 AH

Abdollah Khan served on the Sarakhs frontier and authored a report on **Sarakhs and Naseriyeh Fortress**. The report records the severe conditions of garrison life, including night guard duty, river-work maintenance, fuel collection, and recurring frontier insecurity.

The Sarakhs material also records his administrative recommendations. He criticized inefficient use of state resources, favored militarily useful cavalry over ineffective subsidized households, and linked agricultural self-sufficiency to sustainable frontier defense.

### Naseriyeh and the Sarakhs frontier system

The archive now connects Abdollah Khan to Naseriyeh Fortress, Old Sarakhs, nearby river works and outposts, and the wider Teke/Salur frontier environment. His writing preserves geographic, military, economic, and ethnographic observations while distinguishing his direct observations from information transmitted by local informants.

### Khuzestan and the Lorestan road — approximately 1305–1308 AH

Abdollah Khan later commanded the **Fadavi Regiment** during a major Khuzestan/Lorestan assignment. His surviving report follows military movements and provincial conditions through places including Dezful, Shush, Shushtar, Falahiyah, Ahvaz, Hoveyzeh, and Mohammerah.

The report records:
- military deployment and garrison arrangements;
- road and water conditions;
- bridge and artillery infrastructure;
- irrigation and dam proposals;
- agriculture and long-distance trade;
- Falahiyah political conflict;
- Hoveyzeh ruling families and tribal politics;
- Abdollah Khan's administrative assessments and reform recommendations.

The database does not assign psychological labels to Abdollah Khan. However, his surviving reports repeatedly show him evaluating **military readiness, logistics, infrastructure, settlement, agriculture, trade, and local political power as connected administrative problems**.
