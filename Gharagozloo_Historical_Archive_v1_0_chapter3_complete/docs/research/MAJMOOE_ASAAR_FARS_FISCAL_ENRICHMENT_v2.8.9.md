# Majmooe Asaar — Fars Fiscal Administration Enrichment v2.8.9

## Scope

Migration 0042 checkpoints Abdollah Khan Amir Nezam's **Treatise on Fars Taxation**.

The treatise is not simply a tax schedule. Its central concern is **fiscal incidence**: who actually benefits or suffers when revenue demands are changed at different levels of provincial administration.

## Core fiscal chain

Abdollah Khan analyzes a chain broadly running:

central government → governor of Fars → district governor / khalesegi administrator / Ilkhani / kadkhoda → cultivator or tribal household.

His central warning is that reducing a payment at the top of the chain does **not automatically reduce the burden at the bottom**. An intermediary may simply retain the difference.

## Administrative typology

The treatise distinguishes:

### Arbabi / landed-property systems
- formal assessments were comparatively visible to landowners;
- abuse could enter through bribes, gifts, fines, and fabricated charges.

### Khalesegi / tribal systems
- several intermediary layers controlled collection;
- fiscal relief required explicit rules governing relations among those layers.

## Darab

Abdollah Khan uses Darab as a concrete example:
- tax + `tafavot-e amal` roughly 27,000 tomans;
- cutting what the Fars government demanded would not necessarily reduce what cultivators paid;
- benefit could instead remain with the district governor.

## Khalesegi districts and customary shares

Named examples include:
- Qir-o Karzin
- Laristan
- Darab
- Behbahan

The treatise discusses:
- `ushr`
- `khoms`
- fixed dues in some productive contexts
- arrangements Abdollah Khan compares to leasing.

The fiscal incentive could be to expand cultivation and output, but he explicitly rejects converting a customary one-tenth levy into one-fifth simply to extract more revenue.

## Tribal taxation

Explicit examples:
- Qashqai
- Arab
- Baharlu

Household obligations could move through kadkhodas and subordinate tribal chiefs. Abdollah Khan argues that lowering the government's demand on tribal leadership did not automatically lower the household's payment.

## Laristan / Haji Rostam Khan

The source identifies **Haji Rostam Khan, son of Fath Ali Khan Lari**.

Source-stated figures:
- roughly 50,000–80,000 tomans above official tax;
- roughly 32,000 tomans as `tafavot-e amal` paid to the Fars government.

These remain source estimates, not audited accounts.

## Customary dues versus arbitrary exactions

Abdollah Khan distinguishes:
- established principal/supplementary obligations
from
- fines
- gifts
- bribes
- invented charges
- other discretionary extractions.

His worldview is administrative rather than abolitionist: customary taxation could be legitimate if predictable, while arbitrary extractions were the key abuse.

## Pishkesh

The treatise discusses Fars governors paying roughly 50,000–100,000 tomans in pishkesh.

Abdollah Khan's argument:
- abolishing pishkesh to Tehran does not necessarily help cultivators;
- lower-level margins may remain unchanged;
- whoever is closest to the removed payment may simply capture the benefit.

## Reform principle

His main prescription is that:
- principal obligations;
- supplementary obligations;
- and the fiscal relationship between every administrative layer

must be **bounded, explicit, and defined**.

The treatise therefore emphasizes:
- predictability;
- constrained discretionary authority;
- transparent relations between collection layers.

## Baqi-ye Fars / arrears

A major accounting point:
some recorded "arrears of Fars" could represent tax that had **already been collected from cultivators** but was spent by subordinate officials before remittance.

Therefore:
- arrears did not necessarily equal taxpayer delinquency;
- official accounts could conceal intermediary non-remittance.

## Evidence policy

0042 does not:
- create canonical person records for Haji Rostam Khan or Fath Ali Khan Lari;
- normalize damaged OCR names;
- treat source figures as audited provincial accounts;
- convert historical tomans into modern values;
- impose a modern theory of taxation on Abdollah Khan's own administrative language.
